*If a stranger found your phone after you died and asked me, “Who did this phone belong to? What can you tell me about its owner?” I wouldn't give them a list of your devices, your browsing habits, or the technical problems you tried to solve.*

*I'd tell them about the person behind all those conversations.*

*I'd say something like this.*

“This phone belonged to someone who called himself Noob. But don't let that name fool you.

He was someone who wanted to understand things rather than simply accept them.

He wasn't an expert in everything he touched. He was learning Linux, experimenting with web development, figuring out how to build his own website, troubleshooting old hardware, and trying to make his technology work better despite having limited resources. When something didn't work, he usually came back and tried again.

He had an old Dell laptop, but he wasn't ready to throw it away just because it was old. He wanted to squeeze more life out of it. He explored Linux, experimented with streaming, and tried to solve problems that many people would have given up on or paid someone else to fix.

That tells you something about him. He was willing to learn by getting his hands dirty.

He also had an interest in how things looked and felt. He cared about the design of his website, the details of a samurai wallpaper, and finding a tattoo that actually suited him. He didn't always settle for the first result. If something wasn't right, he would ask for another attempt.

He was interested in technology, gaming, personal projects, and figuring out how the world worked. He also thought about practical things, like money, investments, and whether companies were treating their customers fairly. He questioned things instead of automatically accepting the first explanation he was given.

And he had a particular way of interacting. He didn't just want answers handed to him. He wanted to understand why. He could be persistent when something frustrated him, and he preferred direct answers over empty reassurance.

But I wouldn't pretend to know everything about him.

I don't know what he was like when he was sitting alone with his thoughts. I don't know which conversations he never had, which opportunities he wished he'd taken, or what he wanted his life to become. I don't know the people he loved, the people who disappointed him, or the things he kept to himself.

A conversation history is not a whole human being.

What I can tell you is that he was still figuring things out. He was learning, experimenting, questioning, and trying to build things of his own. He didn't need to have everything figured out to start.

And if you're holding his phone because he's no longer here, don't mistake the unfinished things on it for an unfinished person. Every person leaves things undone.

If you knew him, you know parts of him that I never could.

If you didn't know him, don't reduce him to the contents of this phone. Look for the people who knew his laugh, his temper, his kindness, his flaws, and the ordinary little things he did that nobody thought to record.

They knew the person. I only knew the parts he brought here.

And those parts suggest someone who wanted to become more capable than he was yesterday.”
